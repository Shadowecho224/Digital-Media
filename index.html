Attacks & Combat Techniques

Punches (Chok)

  • Jab Attack Damage: 1d6+18 (Punch) [Mat Nueng]: A quick, straight lead-hand strike used to gauge distance, disrupt an opponent's rhythm, and set up heavier combinations.
  • Cross Attack Damage: 1d6+18 (Punch) [Mat Chong]: A powerful straight punch thrown with the rear hand, driving rotation from the hips and back foot for maximum impact.
  • Hook (Punch) [Mat Weng Kap]: A circular punch targeting the side of the opponent's jaw, cheek, or body, executed with a bent elbow.
  • Uppercut (Punch) [Mat Soei]: An upward-striking punch aimed under the chin or into the solar plexus, usually thrown from a lower, loaded stance.
  • Overhand 1d6+18 (Punch) [Mat Khwang]: A looping, downward-arc punch thrown over an opponent’s guard, leveraging heavy body weight shift.
  • Spinning Back Fist 1d6+18 (Punch) [Mat Klab Thang]: A rotational surprise attack where the fighter spins 360 degrees to strike with the back of the fist or knuckle line.

Kicks (Te)

  • Roundhouse Kick (Kick) [Te Thad]: The quintessential Muay Thai kick, thrown with a fully extended leg using the shinbone (not the foot) as the striking surface, powered by full hip rotation.
  • Low Kick (Kick) [Te Khaa]: A roundhouse kick directed at the opponent's thighs (outer or inner lead leg) designed to damage muscle tissue, compromise movement, and weaken power generation.
  • Middle Kick (Kick) [Te Lam Tor]: A mid-level roundhouse targeting the ribs, liver, and arms, frequently used to score points or weaken an opponent's guard.
  • High Kick (Kick) [Te Kien]: A high-level roundhouse aimed at the neck or head, capable of delivering immediate knockouts.
  • Axe Kick (Kick) [Te Khao]: A vertical downward heel strike aimed at the face or collarbone, brought up high and dropped straight down.
  • Spinning Hook Kick / Wheel Kick (Kick) [Te Klab Thang]: A rotational kick that whipped around to hit the head or body with the heel or sole of the foot.

Push Kicks / Teeps (Thap)

  • Straight Teep (Teep) [Thap Chong]: A front-pushing foot jab used to maintain distance, stop an incoming attack, or push an opponent off balance.
  • Side Teep (Teep) [Thap Khang]: Executed by turning the hip sideways to drive the sole or heel of the foot into the midsection or thigh for extra distance creation.
  • Jumping Teep (Teep) [Thap Loin]: A leaping front push kick designed to gain sudden reach and power against a retreating opponent.

Elbow Strikes (Sok)

  • Horizontal Elbow (Elbow) [Sok Cut]: A parallel slashing motion across the face or jawline, commonly causing cuts or knockouts.
  • Slashing / Diagonal Elbow (Elbow) [Sok Kheng]: A diagonal downward or upward slice across the opponent's guard.
  • Uppercut Elbow (Elbow) [Sok Ngad]: An upward-striking elbow driven directly through the center of an opponent's guard toward the chin.
  • Downward / Chop Elbow (Elbow) [Sok Saab]: A high-to-low vertical chopping motion targeting the top of the forehead, nose, or crown.
  • Spinning Back Elbow (Elbow) [Sok Klab]: A blind-spot rotational attack executed after turning the torso to strike with the point of the elbow.
  • Jumping Elbow (Elbow) [Sok Loin]: A leap into the air delivering a crushing downward elbow strike.

Knee Strikes (Khao)

  • Straight Knee (Knee) [Khao Chong]: Driven forward into the abdomen or solar plexus, powered by driving the hips straight ahead.
  • Diagonal Knee (Knee) [Khao Chiang]: A curved knee strike brought up at an angle to bypass a front guard and target the ribs or liver.
  • Curving / Side Knee (Knee) [Khao Khong]: Swung horizontally around the opponent's body to target the floating ribs or side of the torso during a clinch.
  • Jumping Knee (Knee) [Khao Loin]: A high-flying knee strike launched straight toward the chest or chin of an opponent.
  • Slapping Knee (Knee) [Khao Noi]: Used inside the clinch to hit the inner or outer thigh to wear down legs and break rhythm.

Clinch Work & Grappling (Pam)

  • Full Plum / Main Clinch (Clinch) [Plum Chap Kor]: Control established by locking both hands behind the upper back of the opponent's head, pulling down to control their posture and deliver knees.
  • Double Underhooks / Body Lock (Clinch) Gripping under both of the opponent's arms to control their center of gravity and execute trips or knees to the body.
  • Single Collar Tie / Half Plum (Clinch) One hand behind the neck with the other hand controlling an arm, shoulder, or wrist for mixed striking and breaking balance.
  • Pummeling (Clinch) The fluid hand-fighting process inside the clinch to transition between positions and secure dominant control.

Sweeps & Throws

  • Catch and Sweep (Sweep) Catching an opponent's incoming roundhouse kick and sweeping their standing support leg to send them to the canvas.
  • Clinch Throws / Off-Balancing (Sweep) [Dump]: Turning an opponent's shoulders inside the clinch while stepping around their hips to throw them off balance or toss them to the floor.
  • Foot Sweeps (Sweep) Tipping or kicking the bottom ankle/foot of an opponent when their weight is transitioning.

Defensive Tactics (Pram)

  • Shin Block / Check (Defense) [Bong Te]: Raising the shin to a 45-degree angle to absorb and block middle or low roundhouse kicks with the dense upper shinbone.
  • Lean Back / Evasion (Defense) [หลบ - Lop]: Bending the upper torso backward at the waist to let high kicks or head strikes pass harmlessly overhead without moving foot position.
  • Parrying (Defense) Using the hands or forearms to redirect punches (like jabs and crosses) off line.
  • Catching Kicks (Defense) Trapping incoming body kicks between the arm and torso to set up immediate counter-strikes, sweeps, or takedowns.
  • Footwork and Pivot (Defense) [Yung Wa]: Circling out, stepping off angles, and utilizing rhythm disruptions to slip away from aggressive pressure.
